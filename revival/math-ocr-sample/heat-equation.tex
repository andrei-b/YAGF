\section*{Heat diffusion in a finite rod}
A rod of length $L$ has thermal diffusivity $\alpha>0$.

With both ends held at zero temperature, the heat equation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\alpha\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}
\end{equation}

Separation of variables gives the following eigenvalues.
\begin{equation}
\lambda_n=\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2
\end{equation}

The initial temperature profile $f(x)$ fixes each coefficient.
\begin{equation}
b_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\,dx
\end{equation}

Combining the modes gives the temperature at time $t$.
\begin{equation}
u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\,e^{-\alpha\lambda_n t}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)
\end{equation}
